<h1 style="color: blue;"> When my interest began </h1>

I would like to say that my first exposure and interest to coding began when I was in elementary. It was actually in the form of block coding. There was a website called <a href="https://code.org/en-US">Code.org</a> which had assignments that were related to videogames like Angry Birds or Minecraft. When I got home from school, I redid all of the block code exercises on my laptop and messed around with any variation of statements. I came to find out that there was an option to convert the block code into Java. I didn't understand it. But, that didn't make me completely lose interest. I changed some statements and functions and switched back and forth between the block code and Java to find out what makes certain things work. However, I didn't come to realize that there was a certain syntax you had to follow for Java, so I couldn't really "crack" the code. A year later, a video happened to stumble into my YouTube recommendations and it was about someone making a robot that shines a laser in your eye. I didn't understand what he was saying at all in the video but that made me want to pursue computer science so I could understand what a whole new world could be about. It's safe to say I'm thankful that video came into my recommended page as Code.org wouldn't nearly have been enough to convince me.

<h1 style="color: blue;"> Why Computer Science? </h1>

I wanted to do computer science mainly because I wanted to do something that many people know about but don't know how to explain. It's more so of the "how does it work" rather than the "what does it do" that I want to show to others. Of course, creating a program that works very well is always the final goal but I want to be able to explain to others why computer programs and other computer related things work. I enjoy making people knowledgeable on things that they wouldn't normally have been into at all.

<h1 style="color: blue;"> Goals </h1>

In the future, I want to improve all my knowledge of coding and web design. As of this article, I'd say I only know around 20% of the languages I know (being Java, Python, and C). Expanding my knowledge of these languages will allow me to build real life applications so that people in the real world can use them. I also want to be able to create my own personal projects to help benefit me. Using my previous ICS class knowledge and my future ICS class knowledge, I'll be able to achieve this.
